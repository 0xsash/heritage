# The Rider — `public/models/rider.glb`

Stylised, painterly guild rider for BELFORT: rigged on the project's **shared humanoid skeleton**, with a small
motion library baked in. Everything is regenerated by one script.

## Rebuild

```
tools/blender/fetch_rider_assets.sh <cache-dir>                 # once; about 220 MB of downloads, cached
RIDER_CACHE=<cache-dir> blender -b --python tools/blender/make_rider.py
```

Blender 4.2 LTS, headless, CPU only (Cycles is used just for baking). A full build takes about 4 minutes on
4 cores. `RIDER_CACHE` defaults to `~/.cache/belfort-rider`. MPFB2 is installed into Blender's user
extensions from the cached zip on first run if it is not already there. Optional environment variables
(`RIDER_OUT`, `RIDER_WORK`, `RIDER_FROM`, `RIDER_UNTIL`, `RIDER_REUSE_BAKE`) are described at the top of the script;
with `RIDER_WORK` set, each stage (`human`, `garments`, `mesh`, `paint`, `anim`) saves a `.blend` and can be resumed.
The build is deterministic (fixed random seed).

| Space | glTF: +Y up, the character **faces +Z**, its left is +X, metres, origin on the ground between the feet |
| Height | 1.78 m to the top of the skull (the hair adds about 5 cm: bounding box top is 1.83 m) |
| Nodes | `rider_rig` (armature, identity transform) → skinned meshes `rider_body` and `rider_cloak` |
| `rider_body` | head, hands, eyes, hair, tunic sleeves, jerkin, hose, boots, bracers, belts, straps, buckles, pouches, bell — 24,519 triangles |
| `rider_cloak` | cloak (outer + lining + rim), shoulder mantle, cowl, hood (down), clasp — 7,634 triangles. Separate so the game can hide or restyle it |
| Triangles | **32,153** in total (budget 35,000) |
| Material | one, `rider`: opaque, double-sided, metallic-roughness |
| Textures | 2048², embedded JPEG: `rider_basecolor` (sRGB), `rider_normal` (tangent space, +Y, deliberately mild), `rider_orm` (R occlusion, G roughness, B metallic — one texture used for both `occlusionTexture` and `metallicRoughnessTexture`) |
| Skinning | 58 joints, max 4 influences per vertex, weights normalised; no Draco, no morph targets, no extensions |
| Size | **3.20 MB** including the 7 animation clips |

Hand-painted look: the base colour already contains painted occlusion, fold shading, edge lights and an eye
highlight, so it reads correctly under flat lighting. Use the occlusion channel gently (`aoMapIntensity` around 0.5)
or leave it off. The hair locks share one small gradient strip at the top of the atlas (their UVs overlap on purpose).

## The shared humanoid skeleton

This is MPFB2 / MakeHuman's **"game engine" rig** (Unreal-mannequin style names), unchanged, plus five optional
accessory bones. Any MakeHuman-derived character with the same rig, and anything retargeted to these names, can
share the clips below.

Core bones (53) — required for every character:

`Root`, `pelvis`, `spine_01`, `spine_02`, `spine_03`, `neck_01`, `head`,
`clavicle_l/r`, `upperarm_l/r`, `lowerarm_l/r`, `hand_l/r`,
`thumb_01..03_l/r`, `index_01..03_l/r`, `middle_01..03_l/r`, `ring_01..03_l/r`, `pinky_01..03_l/r`,
`thigh_l/r`, `calf_l/r`, `foot_l/r`, `ball_l/r` (`ball` is the toe bone). `_l` is the character's left (+X).

Accessory bones (5) — rider-specific; characters without a cloak or bell simply omit them (their animation tracks
then bind to nothing; three.js skips them with a console warning, or filter the tracks out when loading):

`cloak1` → `cloak2` → `cloak3` → `cloak4` (parented to `spine_03`), and `bell` (parented to `pelvis`).

Note the capital R in `Root`; every other name is lower case.

`Root` sits on the ground at the origin and is never animated. Bone local axes are Blender's: **+Y runs along the
bone**. Bone lengths, for retargeting: thigh 0.451, calf 0.438, upper arm 0.262, forearm 0.264, pelvis height 0.972.

### Rest (bind) pose

**A-pose.** Upper arms 48.7° below horizontal in the frontal plane; elbows flexed about 45° so the forearms point
forward and down (MakeHuman's default pose); palms facing down and in, fingers relaxed and slightly spread. Legs
straight and slightly apart (ankles 0.39 m apart, thighs about 5° out from vertical), feet pointing forward. Spine
upright, head level.

### Cloak chain

Four bones down the centre of the cloak's back, each about 0.32 m long, from just under the nape
(`cloak1` head at y = 1.460) to the hem. Weights: the top 5 % of the cloak follows `spine_03` only, blending fully
into `cloak1` by 20 % of its length; below that each bone hands over linearly to the next; the hem is 100 %
`cloak4`. The mantle, cowl and hood ride on the shoulders/spine (`clavicle`, `upperarm`, `spine_03`, `neck_01`).

In the joint's own frame (checked both in Blender and in the exported file): local **+X is the character's left**,
+Y points down the cloak, +Z points backwards. So, post-multiplying a cloak joint's quaternion by

* a rotation about local **+X** by a positive angle swings everything below it **backwards / outwards** (wind from the front, speed);
* a rotation about local **+Z** by a positive angle swings it to the character's **right**; negative, to the left.

Every clip already carries a gentle lagged-sine sway on this chain (and a backward stream when running or falling).
For game-driven wind, either add your rotation on top after the mixer has updated, or strip the `cloak*` tracks from
the clips and drive the four bones yourself. The `run` clip uses 4–7° per bone plus a few degrees of swing; `fall` uses 8–24°.

`bell` hangs the brass hand-bell (and its clapper) from the hip belt on the character's left; same axis convention
as the cloak bones (+X rotation swings it backwards). The clips swing it lightly; rotate it yourself to ring it.

## Animation clips

All clips are sampled at **30 fps**, are **in place**, and are named exactly as below. Only `pelvis` has an animated
translation (the natural bob and sway of the hips, and the crouch in jump/land); `Root` stays at the origin, so the
game moves the character. Looping clips repeat the first pose on their last key.

| clip | duration | frames | loops | source (UAL action) | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `idle` | 4.000 s | 120 | yes | `Idle_Loop` | breathing and a small weight shift; the source loop (2.5 s) is slowed to 4 s |
| `walk` | 0.867 s | 26 | yes | `Walk_Loop` | one full cycle (two steps). Feet are planted at **1.5 m/s**; play at `speed / 1.5` |
| `run` | 1.033 s | 31 | yes | `Jog_Fwd_Loop` | one full cycle. Feet are planted at **5.0 m/s**; play at `speed / 5.0` |
| `jump` | 0.500 s | 15 | no | `Jump_Start` (last 70 %) | push-off to the tucked apex pose; hold the last frame or cross-fade to `fall` |
| `fall` | 1.000 s | 30 | yes | `Jump_Loop` | airborne loop, cloak lifted |
| `land` | 0.500 s | 15 | no | `Jump_Land` (first 70 %) | impact crouch and most of the recovery; cross-fade to `idle` |
| `ride_idle` | 4.000 s | 120 | yes | posed in `make_rider.py` | astride a dragon: thighs raised 70° and spread 37°, knees bent 80°, torso leaning 15°, hands forward on the reins; small sway and breathing |

`mount` (optional in the brief) was not made.

How the speeds were set: after retargeting, the script measures how fast the planted foot travels backwards in each
cycle and retimes the cycle so that this equals the target speed. `Walk_Loop` was 1.333 s at 0.99 m/s and is played
1.52× faster; `Jog_Fwd_Loop` was 0.933 s at 5.62 m/s and is played 0.89×. (`Sprint_Loop` plants its feet at 8.5 m/s and
would have needed slowing to 0.59×, so the jog was the better base for 5 m/s.)

How the retarget works (so other characters can reuse it): the library skeleton uses the same bone names in a
T-pose. For each bone and frame the source bone's world-space rotation away from its rest pose is applied to the
matching bone here, after a fixed per-bone swing that accounts for the different rest poses (T-pose there, A-pose
here). Hip translation is scaled by the leg-length ratio (1.06) and shifted so the soles meet the ground.
`stage_anim()` in the script is self-contained and will retarget any other action from the library by adding a line
to `CLIPS`.

## How the model is made

1. **Body** — MPFB2 creates the MakeHuman base mesh (adult male, lean and athletic, with a leaner, stronger-jawed
   face and slightly heroic proportions: broader shoulders, larger hands, feet and head), fits the "game engine"
   rig and loads its skin weights. Everything is scaled to 1.78 m.
2. **Clothes** — modelled in the script, not downloaded: tunic sleeves, jerkin, hose, bracers and the shoulder mantle
   are cut from MakeHuman's clothing-helper shells (which already carry skin weights) and inflated; the jerkin skirt
   is four panels split front, back and sides so it survives the saddle; the cloak is a parametric surface with a few
   large sculpted folds and real thickness (outer, lining, rim); cowl, hood, belts, baldrics, buckles, pouches, bell and
   clasp are built procedurally. Only the boots are a third-party mesh. Hair is a scalp cap plus 58 chunky sculpted locks.
3. **Game mesh** — hidden body geometry is deleted (only head, throat and hands remain), body and boots are decimated,
   everything is unwrapped into one 2048² atlas and joined into the two meshes.
4. **Textures** — position, normal, occlusion, curvature and part-id maps are baked with Cycles; colour, ORM and normal
   maps are then painted procedurally with numpy (three-tone colour blocks, soft gradients, painted occlusion and edge
   lights, rivets, stitching, the embroidered vine trim, eyes, brows, beard shadow).
5. **Animation** — see above.

## Known limitations

* The motion library was authored for a mannequin without a cloak. The cloak chain only carries a procedural sway, so
  in `run`, `fall` and `land` the cloak moves as one fairly stiff sheet, and in deep crouches (`land`) it sticks out behind
  rather than draping over the back.
* `walk` has a slightly hunched, brisk gait (0.65 m steps at about 138 steps per minute at 1.5 m/s). `run` is a jog cycle.
* There is no IK: foot plant was matched by retiming, not by pinning, so expect a few centimetres of slide at speeds
  other than the ones listed, and slight heel sink or float on uneven ground.
* In `ride_idle` the hands are simply held forward with curled fingers; there are no reins to grip, and the back skirt
  panels and cloak hang where a dragon's back will be — they will need to be pushed back by the cloak bones or will
  intersect the saddle.
* The bell and the two pouches are rigid on `pelvis`; a strongly raised left thigh passes through the bell.
* The arms are textured as a tunic sleeve under a bracer; with the arm raised overhead the mantle stretches at the armpit.
* Limb cross-sections are 12–16 sided, which is smooth at gameplay distance but visible in extreme close-up; the hair
  locks are separate interpenetrating shapes rather than one welded sculpt.
* The face has no blend shapes and no jaw or eye bones.
