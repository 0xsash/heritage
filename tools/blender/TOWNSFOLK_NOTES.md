# The townsfolk kit (phase A)

A modular kit of 1650 Brabant townsfolk on the rider's skeleton: bodies, garments, headwear, hair, beards and
hand props, all fitted to every body they are meant for, painted in the rider's house style into shared atlases, and
assembled into 14 archetypes for approval. **Phase A builds the kit and renders the lineup only; nothing is exported
to `public/` yet.** Everything is regenerated by one script.

## Run

```
tools/blender/fetch_townsfolk_assets.sh <cache-dir>       # once; MPFB2 + six CC0 MakeHuman skins (~330 MB download)
TOWNSFOLK_CACHE=<cache-dir> TOWNSFOLK_WORK=<work-dir> blender -b --python tools/blender/make_townsfolk.py
```

Blender 4.2 LTS, headless, CPU only. Stages: `bodies` → `garments` → `mesh` (UV atlases) → `paint` (bake + paint)
→ `assemble` (archetypes), then the preview renders. Each stage saves `<work>/stage_<name>.blend`;
`TOWNSFOLK_FROM=<stage>` resumes after a saved stage, `TOWNSFOLK_UNTIL=<stage>` stops after one.
`TOWNSFOLK_REUSE_BAKE=1` repaints from the cached bake maps (`garment_bake.npz`, `skin_bake.npz`) without re-baking.
`TOWNSFOLK_SHOTS=front,back,faces,sit|none`, `TOWNSFOLK_SAMPLES` (default 48), `TOWNSFOLK_PREVIEW` (default `<work>/preview`)
and `TOWNSFOLK_WALK_FRAME` (default 1, mid-stride) control the previews. MPFB2 is installed into Blender from the cached zip on
first run if missing (same as `make_rider.py`). The rider must exist at `public/models/rider.glb` (the previews take
its clips and stand it at the end of the lineup).

Timing on 4 cores: a full run from scratch takes about 50 min: bodies 15 s, garments ~2 min, atlas 20 s, paint
~15 min (almost all of it the two Cycles AO bakes; ~1 min with `TOWNSFOLK_REUSE_BAKE=1`), assemble ~1 min, previews
~15 min at 64 samples. The build is deterministic. Previews written: `lineup_front.png`, `lineup_back.png` (3200 × 1000),
`face_<old_woman|merchant|beguine|boy>.png` and `faces.png`, `sit.png`, and `pose_check.png` (everyone side-on in walk
×2, talk and sit, for clipping checks).

Code: `tools/blender/make_townsfolk.py` (stage runner) and `tools/blender/townsfolk/`:
`common.py` (settings, helpers shared with `make_rider.py`, the dye mask), `bodies.py`, `garments.py` (garments,
headwear, hair, beards), `props.py`, `atlas.py`, `paint.py`, `assemble.py` (archetypes, delete-mask, pose retarget),
`preview.py` (lights, camera).

## Space and skeleton

Same conventions as the rider (see `RIDER_NOTES.md`): Blender Z up / glTF +Y up, the character faces -Y in Blender
(+Z in glTF), its left is +X, metres, origin on the ground between the feet. Every body carries MPFB2's **"game engine"
rig, unchanged** (the 53 core bones of the shared skeleton, same names, same A-pose rest) plus the rider's optional
`cloak1`..`cloak4` chain under `spine_03`, placed down that body's back from just under the nape to about knee
height. There is no `bell` bone. Rest poses differ only in bone lengths and positions (each body's own proportions).

## How fitting works

Every piece is built by a function that runs once per body it fits:

* **Shell garments** (stockings, breeches, shoes, clogs, boots, doublet, smock, bodice, skirt, apron, habit, buff coat)
  are cut from that body's MakeHuman clothing helpers (`helper-tights`, `helper-skirt`), which have the same topology
  on every body. The cut is evaluated on a fixed **reference body** (`man` for men's and shared pieces, `woman` for
  women's) and applied to the same edges on every body, so **a garment has identical vertices, faces and UVs on every
  body**; only positions differ. Inflation, flare, pleats and rims are applied per body.
* **Parametric pieces** (collars, kerchief, capes, hoods, veils, the huik, hair, beards, belts, sash) use fixed grids:
  a ring walk over the outermost layer below (collars and capes lie on the doublet / bodice / habit), a spherical grid
  over the skull (coif, veil, huik hood, hair), or a hanging drape that is pushed out of the body (huik, long hair).
* **Hats and helmets** are lathed and scaled to the head; rigid on `head`.

Skin weights: MakeHuman's tights / skirt helper weights by data transfer (the skirt helper's weights are designed for
skirts, so long skirts, habits and coat skirts bend with both thighs and stretch between them rather than tear);
headwear is rigid on `head` blending into the neck/shoulders below the jaw; the huik's long fall hands over to
`cloak1..4`; sash tails and the friar's rope ends ride on `pelvis` + one thigh. Max 4 influences, normalised.

**Delete-mask (hidden body):** for each outfit, body faces whose outward ray meets one of the outfit's garments within
that garment's distance (3–6 cm for hair and headwear, 10–16 cm for breeches and smock skirts, 40 cm for skirts and
habits; capped at 6 cm above the waist and 1.8 cm round the neck, so open necklines never show a hole) are removed
(`assemble.hide_covered`). Hats get the same treatment for hair: hair faces above the brim are dropped
(`assemble.hair_under_hat`). Phase B turns this into per-garment body-region masks (see the plan).

## Dye mask convention (COLOR_0)

Every garment, hair, beard and prop mesh carries a vertex colour `dye` (exported as `COLOR_0`):

* **R = 1** — the vertex takes the dye: final base colour = texture × dye colour.
* **R = 0** — keep the painted colour (buttons, brass, buckles, steel, white collars, cuffs, coifs, veils, kerchiefs,
  leather, buff coat, shoes, clogs, wood, rope, straw, bread, beads).
* G, B reserved (0); A = 1.

Dyeable parts are painted in light, slightly warm neutral tones (mid-tone ≈ sRGB `#c4bdb2`, ≈ 0.55 linear) with all
their shading painted in, so a dye colour `c` (linear) lands on the swatch when you multiply by `c / 0.55`
(`assemble.dye_lin`). Hair and beards are dyeable too (one painted grey-brown, dyed brown, black, blond, grey, white).
The previews read the dye from the object property `dye_rgb`; in three.js multiply `material.color` (or a
per-instance colour) where `COLOR_0.r` is 1.

Palette used in the lineup (sRGB): madder `#9a3226`, dark madder `#6e2219`, woad `#41618a`, undyed linen `#e2d6bc`,
russet `#86522e`, black (the wealthy) `#262326`, beguine black `#1f1c1e`, Capuchin brown `#6a4f34`, grey `#7a756c`,
moss `#56663e`, ochre `#b89040`, brown `#5e4430`, slate `#4a5058`.

## Textures and the painted look

| atlas | size | contents |
|---|---|---|
| `kit_basecolor` / `kit_orm` / `kit_normal` | 2048² each | every garment, headwear, hair, beard and prop (one shared atlas; UVs authored on the reference fit and shared by all fitted copies) |
| `kit_skin_basecolor` / `kit_skin_orm` / `kit_skin_normal` | 2048² each | the nine bodies, one 682² tile each (MakeHuman's UV layout repacked once with the head 2.6× and hands 1.4× larger), eyes included |

ORM is the rider's layout (R occlusion, G roughness, B metallic). Painting follows `make_rider.py`: position,
normal, curvature, occlusion, part id and garment id are baked with Cycles (each garment baked next to a copy of its
reference body, so the body's occlusion is painted into collars, cuffs and sleeves), then numpy paints three-tone
colour blocks, cool painted occlusion, crease shading from curvature, warm edge light, soft vertical folds on long
garments and a few painted details (bodice lacing and stomacher, skirt hem band, doublet seams and tabs, breeches
garters, shoe rosettes, rope twist, basket weave, wood grain). Skins: per-body skin tone, blush, strong simple brows,
lash line and lid shadow, lips, stubble on the men, forehead / nasolabial / crow's-feet lines and age spots on the old
bodies, painted irises with a highlight; the CC0 MakeHuman skin photo is used only, heavily blurred, as a tonal guide.

## Known limitations (phase A)

* **No per-body animation yet**: the previews retarget single frames. Feet are grounded per frame, not pinned; the
  rider's walk is a brisk, slightly hunched gait, which suits some townsfolk less (old people, the beguine).
* **Mesh weight is not game-ready**: garments and bodies are at build resolution (≈ 137 k unique triangles; a full
  body is 26.8 k triangles before hiding). Decimation and quantisation are phase B (see below).
* **Atlas packing is wasteful**: smart-project cuts many small islands (hair locks, beads, rivets), so texel density
  is lower than it could be and seams are frequent; hair locks should share a gradient strip as on the rider.
* **Delete-mask is per outfit and ray-based** (a few slivers of skin can show at sleeve/neck openings in strong poses);
  phase B should store per-garment body-region masks instead.
* **Layering is by construction order**: collars, kerchief, belts, apron, sash and capes are fitted over one specific
  under-layer (doublet, bodice, smock, skirt, buff coat, habit). Worn over a different top they may sit too tight or too
  loose; the manifest must record these dependencies.
* **Props are rigid on a hand** with no hand pose; hanging props (basket, lantern, bundle) swing rigidly with the hand
  and can touch the skirt; staffs need a holding pose (phase B: a `hold_staff` variant of walk/idle).
* **Long garments in motion**: skirts, habits and the huik follow MakeHuman's skirt weights / the cloak chain; a long
  stride can push a knee through the front of a habit; no cloth simulation.
* **The beguine's wimple** was rebuilt in phase B as one closed panel round the face and under the chin.
* **Faces** are painted on a 682² tile per body (face ≈ 120 px across): enough at 5–20 m, soft in the close-ups.
  There are no blend shapes, jaw or eye bones.
* **Hats over hair**: hats are sized 10–12 % over the head and the preview drops hair faces above the brim; in the game
  this becomes a per-hat hair mask (or a "hat hair" variant of each style).

## Phase B: the game kit (`public/models/townsfolk.glb` + `townsfolk.json`)

Stages are now `bodies → garments → game → mesh → paint → anim → assemble → export` (previews render after
`assemble`, before `export`, because the export simplifies the materials in place). The export writes the two files
in `public/models/` (override with `TOWNSFOLK_GLB` / `TOWNSFOLK_JSON`).

### Game meshes (`townsfolk/game.py`)
* **Regions.** Every body vertex carries a region code (`_REGION`, float): face, ears, scalp, neck, chest_upper, chest,
  belly, hips, upperarm, forearm_up, forearm_low, hand, thigh, knee, shin, foot (symmetric: one code for both sides).
  Hair carries `hair_crown` (above the hat band) / `hair_low`. Codes are in the manifest's `regions`.
* **Always-covered skin is deleted**: chest, belly, hips and thighs are covered by every outfit in the kit, so they are
  not shipped. If a future outfit shows them, drop them from `game.ALWAYS_HIDDEN`.
* **`hides`** per piece come from the phase-A ray test on the piece's reference fit (a region counts when ≥ 90 % of its
  faces are covered), plus a few unions the ray test cannot see (`EXTRA_HIDES`: stockings → shin, hair → scalp; hats →
  `hair_crown`). Face and neck are never hidden, so necklines never open a hole. Rule (as the runtime does it): a
  triangle is dropped when **all three** of its vertices are in hidden regions.
* **Decimation once, re-expressed everywhere.** Each piece is decimated on its reference fit; every kept vertex is stored
  as a barycentric point (plus normal offset) on the full-resolution reference triangle it came from and evaluated on
  each body's full-resolution copy; skin weights are interpolated from that copy. Every copy of a piece therefore has
  one topology, one UV set and one COLOR_0 — the export stores those once and only POSITION / NORMAL / weights per body.
  The body is decimated the same way (face and hands kept denser).

### Clips (`townsfolk/anim.py`): one shared set on `rig.man`
The rider's own clips from `public/models/rider.glb` (idle, walk, walk_formal, talk, sit, run) are retargeted in world
space onto the `man` body's rig; `walk_old`, `look_around` and `point` are derived from them. In place, 30 fps; tracks
are joint **rotations** plus the **pelvis translation** only (no Root motion, no other translation or scale tracks).
**Other bodies: ship one set, scale the pelvis translation track by `bodies[b].pelvisScale`** (pelvis height ratio to
`man`), which is what `TownsfolkKit.clip()` does. `stride` = metres one loop covers on the `man` body (1.70 m), from the
rider's planted-foot speeds scaled by leg length (walk / walk_formal / run) or measured (walk_old).

| clip | duration | loop | stride per loop on `man` | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `idle` | 4.000 s | yes | – | rider `idle` |
| `walk` | 0.867 s | yes | 1.227 m (1.42 m/s) | rider `walk` (planted at 1.5 m/s on the rider) |
| `walk_formal` | 1.000 s | yes | 1.227 m (1.23 m/s) | rider `walk_formal` |
| `walk_old` | 1.133 s | yes | 0.695 m (0.61 m/s) | derived from `walk`: 1.3× slower, leg/arm swing 70 %, 14° stoop, head kept up, knees bent |
| `talk` | 2.933 s | yes | – | rider `talk` |
| `sit` | 3.333 s | yes | – | rider `sit` (seat ≈ 0.42 m × pelvisScale) |
| `run` | 1.033 s | yes | 4.875 m (4.72 m/s) | rider `run` (for fleeing) |
| `look_around` | 4.000 s | yes | – | derived from `idle`: head/neck/chest turn ±48° |
| `point` | 2.500 s | no | – | derived from `idle`: right arm raised to point and lowered |

No sword clips (townsfolk do not fight).

### Export layout
* Scene roots: `folk.<body>` (one per body; extras `{"kit":"group","body":b}`) and `prop.<name>`.
* Under `folk.<body>`, as **siblings**: the armature `rig.<body>` (extras `{"kit":"rig","body":b}`, joints named as in
  rider.glb) and that body's skinned meshes `body.<body>` (extras `{"kit":"body","body":b}`, eyes included) and every
  fitted piece `<piece>.<body>` (extras `{"kit":"piece","piece":p,"body":b}`). The meshes are deliberately *not*
  children of the armature node: `TownsfolkKit.tag()` copies a node's tag to its whole subtree before visiting the
  children, so pieces nested under the armature would all be tagged `rig`.
* Props: unskinned, origin at the grip, +Y up the object, extras `{"kit":"prop","prop":name}`. Grips per body in the
  manifest (`fist_r`, `hang_r`, `hang_l`): position + quaternion relative to the hand joint, chosen so that in `idle` a
  hanging prop hangs straight down and a staff stands upright.
* Materials `kit` and `skin`, embedded JPEG: base colour 2048², ORM and normal 1024² for each.
* COLOR_0 (uint8 normalised RGBA) on pieces and props only; `_REGION` on bodies and hair.
* Post-pass (`export.postprocess`, no third-party tools): KHR_mesh_quantization (normals int8, UVs uint16), weights and
  colours uint8, identical buffer views and accessors shared (indices, UVs and COLOR_0 are stored once per piece for
  all its bodies), translation/scale channels other than the pelvis' removed.

**Bone names (please read):** GLTFLoader gives every node a unique name across the whole file, so only one armature's
joints keep the plain names; the others arrive as `pelvis_1`, `spine_01_3`, ... (the loader keeps the original in
`node.userData.name`). The shared clips only bind, and `getObjectByName('hand_r')` only finds the hand, after the
names are restored. Before constructing `TownsfolkKit`, run:

```ts
const orig = new Map<string, string>();
gltf.scene.traverse((o) => { if ((o as THREE.Bone).isBone && o.userData.name) { orig.set(o.name, o.userData.name); o.name = o.userData.name; } });
for (const c of gltf.animations) for (const t of c.tracks) { const i = t.name.lastIndexOf('.'); const n = orig.get(t.name.slice(0, i)); if (n) t.name = n + t.name.slice(i); }
```

### Known limitations (phase B)
* **Bone names need the small shim above** (GLTFLoader's unique naming); without it only one body animates.
* Rigid props swing with the hand in walk (no hand-hold poses); the staff tilts across the body while walking.
* Decimation leaves a few small holes / jagged edges on thin double-sided pieces (shirt cuffs, kerchief edge).
* Hair is broader and flatter than in phase A but the children's and cropped styles still read as clumps up close.
* `walk_old` is derived procedurally from the rider's walk, not mocap; `look_around` / `point` are procedural.
* The feet are deleted from the bodies (every outfit has shoes, clogs or boots); a barefoot outfit would need them back.

## Phase C: unique faces, enemies, named NPCs

### Face morph targets
Every `body.<body>` mesh carries the same glTF morph targets, in the same order (`mesh.extras.targetNames`, so
three.js fills `morphTargetDictionary`). Each is the half-difference of a MakeHuman `incr` / `decr` detail-target pair
(MPFB's CC0 targets, read from the add-on's data), times a gain, so +1 is about the `incr` shape and −1 about the
`decr` one. Defined in `bodies.FACE_MORPHS`; ranges in the manifest's `faceMorphs`.

| target | +1 … −1 | MakeHuman targets | gain | safe range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nose_length` | longer, drooping … short, turned up | nose-scale-vert, nose-point | 1.0 | −1 … 1 |
| `nose_width` | broad, flared … narrow | nose-scale-horiz, nose-flaring | 1.4 | −1 … 1 |
| `nose_bridge` | humped, prominent … flat, scooped | nose-hump, nose-scale-depth | 1.0 | −1 … 1 |
| `jaw_width` | broad jaw … narrow jaw | chin-bones, chin-width | 1.0 | −1 … 1 |
| `chin_length` | long, forward chin … short, receding | chin-height, chin-prominent | 1.0 | −1 … 1 |
| `cheeks` | full … hollow | l/r-cheek-volume | 1.3 | −1 … 1 |
| `brow` | heavy, low brow … high, flat | eyebrows-trans forward/back + down/up | 1.0 | **−0.6** … 1 (below −0.6 the forehead ridges) |
| `eyes` | larger … smaller | l/r-eye-scale | 1.6 | −1 … 1 |
| `mouth_width` | wide … narrow | mouth-scale-horiz | 1.5 | −1 … 1 |
| `lips` | full … thin | mouth-upper/lowerlip-volume | 1.6 | −1 … 1 |
| `face_fat` | fleshy face and jowls … gaunt | head-fat | 1.5 | −1 … 1 |

Largest displacement per target is 4–15 mm at weight 1. The deltas are stored sparse (only the head moves) as
normalised int16 (1 unit = 1/32767 m): about 0.2 MB for all bodies and pieces.

**Fit of headwear and facial hair: matching morphs.** Pieces that frame the face carry the *same* targets (same
names and order; `"morphs": true` in the manifest) so one set of weights drives body and pieces together, which
`TownsfolkKit` already does through `morphTargetDictionary`. Their deltas are the skin's own deltas at the nearest
point of that body's surface, fading out between 3.5 and 8 cm from the skin (`game.FullBody.sample`). They are:
`coif`, `beguine_veil`, `huik`, `capuche`, `pelerine`, `collar_band`, `kerchief`, `bands`, all hair (`hair_*`) and
all beards (`beard_*`, `moustache`). Rigid headwear has none and needs none (the targets do not move the skull):
`hat_wide`, `hat_pilgrim`, `cap_flat`, `morion`, `skullcap`, `plume`. Body garments have none.

### Skin tone
Body meshes now have `COLOR_0`: r = 1 on skin, 0 on the lips and the eyeballs (brows and lashes are painted in the
skin texture and take the tone with the skin). `skinTones` in the manifest: eight linear-RGB multipliers from pale to
weathered. Note: a body mesh rendered with the stock glTF material would be multiplied by COLOR_0 (black lips and
eyes), so the body always needs the toned material (`TownsfolkKit.toned`), also for the neutral tone.

### Clips added (same shared set, same rules)
`sword_idle` (2.5 s, loop), `attack1` (0.667 s), `attack2` (0.533 s), `hit` (0.4 s), `death` (1.6 s; hold the last
frame) are the rider's clips, retargeted like the others (the fist closes in them). `aim` (3.0 s, loop) and `pray`
(4.0 s, loop) are posed in `anim.py` from the idle: `aim` turns the body a quarter right, levels the right forearm
(the musket lies along it) at chest/shoulder height, brings the left hand forward under the barrel; `pray` is
standing, hands together before the chest, head bowed (not kneeling).

### Props and grips added
* `sword`: plain backsword (0.86 m blade, cross-guard with a knuckle bow), grip **`sword_r`**: fixed in the fist with
  the blade along the fist's axis (little finger → index), as the rider's sword; not straightened by gravity.
* `musket`: matchlock caliver, 1.2 m, grip **`along_r`**: held at the wrist of the stock, the barrel pointing where
  the fingers point, the butt lying back along the forearm. (The brief said `fist_r`; that grip is the upright one
  used by staffs, so the two weapon grips got their own kinds. `TownsfolkKit` reads `props[p].grip` generically.)

### Pieces added
`skullcap` (men; clergy; wear with `hair_thin`), `bands` (men; white clerical collar with two tabs; requires
`habit`), `plume` (men; ostrich feather; requires `hat_wide`; dyeable), `bandolier` (men; leather strap with six
wooden charges; requires `doublet`, excludes `buff_coat`, `sash`, `pelerine`).

### Archetypes
Every archetype now has an explicit `"crowd"` flag: `true` on the 16 street archetypes, `false` on the two new enemy
templates `deserter` (man, man_young, man_stout: doublet or smock in dull dyes, optional buff coat, battered hat or cap
or bare head, boots, beards; sword, musket or nothing) and `musketeer` (man, man_young: wide hat, optional plume,
doublet, optional falling band, bandolier, boots; musket). Both have role `soldier`.

### Other changes in phase C
* Collars, kerchief, bonnet and shirt cuffs are built at game resolution as single layers and are no longer decimated;
  buttons, cuffs, the plume and the bandolier's charges are protected from decimation (`keep` attribute); the open
  edges of coif, veil and huik hood are protected too, so the face openings stay clean curves.
* The wimple/veil is a single layer (the sliver at its side came from decimating a thin double shell).
* Hair: locks are blunter; a hat now hides every lock that reaches above the hat band (`hair_crown` is assigned per
  lock), so no hair pokes through crowns or brims.
* Export: unreferenced animation data is no longer written and joint rotations are stored as normalised int16
  quaternions (core glTF), which more than paid for the morphs and the seven new clips.

### Known limitations (phase C)
* The morphs are MakeHuman's detail targets: they change who someone is at 2–10 m, but two people of the same body
  with middling weights still look related; the texture (brows, age lines) is shared per body.
* `aim` is a levelled-musket pose at chest/shoulder height with both hands on the gun; the cheek is not on the stock.
  Walking with the musket carries it along the forearm, muzzle down and forward.
* `pray` is standing only. `hair_bun` has a hard hairline when worn bare-headed (it is meant to sit under a coif).
* A small dark flap can show at the corner of the huik's hood by the cheek; the hat band keeps a few small bumps.
* The plume is a simple curved ribbon; the bandolier is fitted over the doublet only.
