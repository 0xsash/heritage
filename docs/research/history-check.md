# BELFORT history check: names and facts for Diest, 1650

The owner asked: "Was 'Wouter' a name that appeared back then? Make sure that's all accurate." This file checks every personal name and every statement a player could take as history. Each name and claim is checked against sources opened during this check (listed at the end as S1, S2, ...). No source file was changed.

Verdicts:
- **Names:** fine / change spelling / replace / not verified.
- **Facts:** correct / wrong / imprecise / not verified.

"Not verified" means I found no source that settles the point. It does not mean the point is false.

**The short answer on Wouter: yes.** The Leuven university matriculation register (Rijksarchief Leuven, indexed on Open Archives) lists at least seven students **born in Diest** with the Latin form of the name, Walterus or Gualterus, between 1621 and 1655. One of them is "Walterus Nicolai", born in Diest and enrolled in **1650** ([record](https://www.openarchieven.nl/abl:bb81d2b2-d91b-ca2e-f8d3-83a88a419be5)). The others are Gualterus Qums (1621), Walterus Mannaerts (1631), Walterus van Hamell (1639), Walterus Minten (1642), Walterus Rutten (1653) and Gualterus Remens (1655). Registers wrote Latin; people said **Wouter**. The patronymic surname *Wouters* comes from that spoken form, and the same register has students called Wouters from Antwerp and the Kempen (S37). "Wouter Claes" is a sound name for a Diest toll-keeper in 1650.

## The ten most important corrections

1. **The Galgenberg was not Diest's gallows.** The heritage inventory calls it "de terechtstellingsplaats van de schepenbank van Kaggevinne", about 2 km south-west of Diest (S10). The game says "Diest hangt zijn veroordeelden op deze heuvel" (lore.ts:148), and several lines repeat it.
2. **Gate names.** The medieval gates of Diest were the Beverenpoort (towards Hasselt and Maastricht), Putterstraatpoort (towards Leuven and Aarschot), Allerheiligenpoort (towards Zichem), Schaluinpoort (towards Antwerp and the Kempen) and Schaffensepoort. The **Koepoort** was one of five small gates that were walled up after 1508 (S2). The game's "Leuvense / Antwerpse / Maastrichtse Poort" are later names taken from the Ferraris map (1770s). The game's use of the Koepoort as a working cattle gate in 1650 is probably wrong. The "Demerpoort" is not in the inventory list at all.
3. **Prices are about twenty times too high.** Shop prices are in *florijnen* (gamehud.ts shows "florijnen"). An apple, a waffle and a pint each cost 1 florijn (20 stuivers), and a bed costs 4 florijnen, while the game's own glossary says a day's wage is 10 to 12 stuivers (kroniek.ts:219). A pilgrim even says "Twaalf stuivers voor een bed" (diest_talk.ts:229). Show shop prices in stuivers, or lower them.
4. **No carillon in Diest in 1650.** The carillon in the crossing tower ("de Mosterdpot") was installed in **1671**, with bells by Pieter Hemony (S4). The class "Beiaardier" and the line "Mijn vader was beiaardier" are anachronistic for Diest. Use *klokluider* or *klokkenist*, or keep the class and say the player learned the craft in Mechelen or Antwerp.
5. **Holle Griet is not verified.** The only mention I found is one unsourced sentence in a 's-Hertogenbosch heritage leaflet about cannons named "Griet": "In Diest, in België, staat de 'Holle Griet', een bombarde van 5.000 kg … 1465" (S22). No Diest or Flemish source confirms it. Either relabel the entry *verzonnen* or remove the claim.
6. **The Scherpenheuvel oak: date, and who ordered it.** The KNOB study (S16) says the oak had already been felled by order of the Antwerp bishop Miraeus before the archdukes' first visit in 1604, "vermoedelijk om bijgelovige praktijken in de kiem te smoren". English Wikipedia says archbishop Hovius ordered it (S17). The firm date is **1602**: the year the statue was moved out of the oak into a wooden chapel (S17). Say "kort na 1600" or "rond 1602", not "in 1602 geveld" as a fixed fact.
7. **Scherpenheuvel is a seven-sided fortified town, not a "seven-pointed star".** The town, its streets and its walls follow a heptagon. The seven-pointed star was the pattern of trees in the walled garden (hortus conclusus) around the church (S16). Building began in 1605 and the first stone of the church was laid on 2 July 1609 (S16, S17), so "veertig jaar geleden" should be "een halve eeuw geleden".
8. **Where Kludde tales were recorded.** De Cock (1921) gives Wichelen, Herdersem, Lebbeke, Uitbergen and Aalst, with water-devil variants at Wetteren and Nevele (S32). Dutch Wikipedia adds Dendermonde, Hemiksem, Schelle, Dilbeek, Zemst and Weerde (S31). I found nothing for **Merchtem**. The Oschaert belongs to **Hamme**, near Dendermonde (S34).
9. **Words that did not exist in 1650.** "kittens" is English (diest_talk.ts:193). "Race!" dates from 1827–30 (S43). "baby" dates from 1852 (S41). "geen cent": the cent coin dates from 1816 (S42). Also "Kamillethee": the word *thee* is first recorded in 1637, and only for real tea (S40).
10. **Cloth and Cologne.** Diest cloth was sold "op vrijwel alle grote West-Europese markten" in the 14th and 15th centuries. A severe crisis followed in the mid-15th century (S6), and by the end of the 17th century the trade had "volledig uitgestorven" (S5). No source I found names Cologne. Present-tense lines such as "een koper in Keulen vertrouwt het" (kroniek.ts:56) describe 1450, not 1650.

The next five, in order of importance:

11. "Gebouwd … over twee mensenlevens": Sint-Sulpitius took more than two centuries, 1321–1534 (S4).
12. "Sint-Trudokerk … toegewijd aan Sint-Truiden": the saint is Sint-Trudo. Sint-Truiden is the town named after him.
13. The 1648 return of the Hof van Nassau, and Orange's officers coming back after 1648, are not verified.
14. The "Stadhuis" of 1650 was the medieval *schepenhuis*. The present town hall dates from the 1720s (S14).
15. In 1650 Scherpenheuvel was served by the **Oratorians**, founded for it in 1624 by archbishop Boonen (S12). The "priester en gids" there should be an Oratorian father (*pater*).

## 1. Names

Most evidence for men's names comes from one source: the Leuven matriculation register (S37). I took every student enrolled 1580–1680 under the Latin form of a name and read each one's recorded birthplace. "Diest n" means n students born in Diest. "Region" means birthplaces in the Hageland, the Kempen, Leuven, Mechelen, Antwerp, Lier, Tienen or Sint-Truiden. This register only covers men, and mostly the better-off, and it is written in Latin. A miss in this register is weak evidence that a name was absent. A hit is strong evidence that it was present.

For women's names I found no local register I could search. The evidence there is general:
- Grammarian Van Heule (1625): "de Brabanders … zeggende Het manneken, Het wijfken". The *-ken* ending is recorded in Brabant villages in 1619–1672 (S38).
- Brabant women with *-ken* names: Mayken Verhulst (Mechelen, c. 1518) and her daughter Mayken (S39).

For most female names, the Diest-specific verdict is therefore "not verified locally".
