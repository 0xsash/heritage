# Belfort: the long story

The owner chose the ending on 21 November 2026: **De Waterwolf** as the spine, **De Klokkengieter** as the human
enemy of the middle chapters, and **De Gulden Draak** guarding the last belfry. This file works backwards from
that ending. Everything a later chapter needs is listed under the chapter that has to plant it.

Writing rules are in `voice.md`. Facts marked (check) have not been through `docs/research/history-check.md` yet.

## 1. What is really going on

The player learns this in pieces. No character knows all of it before chapter 6.

- **The land is borrowed.** The first dike builders could not beat the water, so they struck a bargain: the land
  is a loan, and the loan runs for as long as its hours are counted. The bells count the hours. Where a town's
  bells fall silent, the count stops, the loan falls due there, and the water may come and take it back.
- **Before the bells there were dragons.** They guarded the land against the water. Bells were cheaper and more
  obedient, and their sound puts every old thing to sleep, the guardians included. Bells and dragons do the same
  work and cannot share a sky. That is why the hero's dragon woke the night Diest fell silent, why he came to the
  one who had just lost someone to the water, and why every bell the hero restores costs him.
- **The water does not hate. It collects.** It is patient and exact. It keeps what it takes, and it keeps a child
  to do its counting. Neelke counts everything. She is alive, and she is the water's **Telster**.
- **The Kludde is a servant, not the master.** He stole the clappers of Diest because a mute bell is scrap, and
  scrap can be carted away without anyone ringing the alarm.
- **A man is carting them away.** Meester Lenaert, called the Klokkengieter, casts the crown's cannon in
  Mechelen. By the law of war the master of the artillery may claim the bells of a taken town (check:
  klokkenrecht). He takes more than the law gives. His family's village lies under the Scheldt, its bell with
  it; to him a bell is a prayer that did not work and a cannon is an answer. He thinks he is using the water.
  The water is using him.

## 2. The ending (chapter 7)

Ghent, then the dikes of the Scheldt.

1. **The Belfort of Ghent.** Klokke Roeland is the last bell that still counts for the whole county. On top of
   the tower sits the gilded dragon, there since 1377. He is not a statue. He was the dragon of the last rider,
   who stood where the hero stands and chose the bell; her dragon was not put to sleep but gilded and bound to
   guard it. He has had almost three hundred years to regret it. **Boss: De Gulden Draak**, in the air around
   the tower. He is not trying to kill the hero. He is trying to find out whether the hero means it.
2. **The Waterwolf.** Every bell downstream of Ghent is silent. The flood comes up the Scheldt from the drowned
   land of Saeftinghe, in the shape of what it has taken: wreckage, drowned church towers, the drowned Ros
   Beiaard, and inside it the bell of Saeftinghe still ringing under water (check: the legend). Neelke speaks
   for it. **Final boss: De Waterwolf**, fought from the dragon over the breaking dikes. It cannot be killed.
   It can be held long enough to choose.
3. **The choice, at Roeland.**
   - **Ring.** The count is fixed for good and the water is bound. Every old thing sleeps and does not wake.
     The hero's dragon climbs the Belfort and turns to gilt where the old one sat; the old one is finally
     allowed to die. Neelke walks out of the water, and never stops counting.
   - **Pay.** The hero refuses the bell. The land that is owed goes back to the sea, and how much is owed is
     what the ledger says (section 3). People lose their homes; the hero watches which. The dragon stays, and
     more dragons will wake. Neelke stays with the water until the debt is counted down, by her own choice.
   - **Recast** (earned, hidden). Only if the gun De Stomme was kept whole in chapter 5, the ledger is close to
     balance, and the Oude Volk trust the hero: Roeland and De Stomme are cast into one new bell that rings with
     the tide. The water gets the tidal lands each day and gives them back; the guardians stay awake; nobody
     gets everything. Neelke comes home with the tide and goes out with it.
4. **Epilogue by faction.** One scene and a few lines that depend on the hero's best faction and on the choice.

## 3. The ledger (De Rekening)

One number carries the choice through the whole game, so the ending is something the player did, not picked.

- Every town has a bell. In every chapter the hero decides whether that bell rings again.
- **A bell that rings:** the town is safe from what woke there, De Kerk and De Gilden gain trust, and the dragon
  loses something for good (a little of a power, never the whole).
- **A bell left silent:** the dragon stays whole, Het Oude Volk gain trust, the old things stay awake around
  that town (harder roads, richer hunting, a world boss that stays), and the water advances one step there.
- Neelke's tallies appear wherever the hero decides: five strokes and a bar, on a post, a quay, a door.
- The ledger sets how much land is owed in the "Pay" ending, how strong the dragon is at the end, which allies
  come to the last fight, and whether "Recast" is open.
- Diest's bells already ring (chapter 1 cannot be undone). It is the first entry, and the hero did not know he
  was making it. That is the point of the scene where the dragon's eyes fall shut.

## 4. The people

- **The hero and the dragon.** The dragon never speaks. What he wants shows in what he does.
- **Neelke, the Telster.** Seen in chapter 3 at a distance, met in chapter 4, speaks plainly in chapter 6. She is
  not bewitched. She has seen the account and thinks it is fair. She is eight and she is right about the sums.
- **Meester Lenaert, the Klokkengieter.** Enemy of chapters 2 to 5. Courteous, exact, grieving, and legally in
  the right more often than the player would like. Never a cackling villain.
- **The rider of 1377.** Known only from what she left: a name scratched in the Belfort, the gilt dragon.
- **The mute boy of Diest**, who came back from the water and lays stones in a row. The water sent him back
  with a message nobody has learned to read. Reading it is a chapter 3 quest.
- **Het Oude Volk.** They want the guardians back. They do not want the sea. When the water rises in chapter 6
  they split, and the hero's standing decides which half he gets.
- **Returning faces:** Pieter the sexton, the beguine of the infirmary, Marieke the pedlar (in every town, since
  she walks every road), the drossaard.

## 5. Chapters

Each chapter is a region with a main line of six to eight quests, six to ten side quests, town life in each
town, a chapter boss, one or two world bosses, a bell decision, and one beat each for Neelke and the dragon.
Target: one and a half to two hours each, ten to twelve hours in all.

Chapters 2 and 3 can be played in either order: Diest lies on the border, the oak figure hums west to Leuven,
and from the east come carts of people from Loon whose bells were taken. Both are needed to open Mechelen.

**2. Het Land van Loon.** In 1650 this is not Brabant: it belongs to the prince-bishop of Liège, it is neutral,
and every army crosses it anyway (check: Lorraine troops in the 1650s). Its towers were stripped first. The
heath at night belongs to the Wild Hunt. People here have had a year without bells and have learned to live
beside what woke: some badly, some better than before. The Oude Volk are strongest here. The hero finds the
wheel ruts of the bell wagons, all going west. Neelke: tallies on the milestones, fresh. Dragon: he is stronger
here than anywhere, and happier.

**3. Leuven.** The university holds the oldest bell registers. A canon and a student who do not agree help the
hero read them: bells are entered like debts. The towers of Sint-Pieter were meant to be the highest in the
world and were never finished because the ground would not carry them (check). Diest, Leuven, Mechelen: the
water does not let Brabant finish its towers. The legend of Fiere Margriet, the drowned girl the Dijle carried
upstream in a ring of light (check), is the one time the water gave somebody back, and why is the chapter's
question. The mute boy's stones are read here: they are a date. Neelke: seen across the Dijle, counting boats.

**4. Mechelen.** The city of bell founders and of the royal gun foundry (check). The hero gets inside by way of
a faction: the guild, the garrison, the smugglers who carry the bronze, or the canons who want their bells back.
Here bells become cannon, and here the hero meets Lenaert, who shows him the account books and is not lying.
Boss: Het Gietsel, a thing of fused, half-melted bells that the foundry woke without meaning to. Neelke: the
hero speaks to her for the first time. She asks him how many bells he has rung. Dragon: he will not land in the
city; the first time he refuses the hero.

**5. Antwerpen.** The Dutch have kept the Scheldt shut since 1585 and the peace of 1648 sealed it; the greatest
port of the north is dying of it. Lenaert holds the citadel with soldiers who have not been paid, and De Stomme,
cast from the bells of a hundred towers, points down the river at the Dutch forts. Firing it would open the
river and the war again, and its report would stop every bell that hears it. The guilds of Antwerp want it
fired. So may the player. Boss: Lenaert and his gun crews on the citadel walls, on foot and from the air.
Decision: sink De Stomme in the river, or keep it whole. Lenaert's end depends on the factions; none of the
ends is a triumph.

**6. Dendermonde.** Kludde country, and the place where Charlemagne had the horse Beiaard drowned (check). The
water is rising with no rain. The Oude Volk split. The Kludde returns at home and far larger. Neelke comes
ashore and tells the hero all of section 1, and asks him to stop. The drowned Ros Beiaard rises. Dragon: the
first time he is afraid.

## 6. What earlier chapters must plant

**Chapter 1 and the opening (changes to existing content):**
- A clerk of the artillery in Diest who asks, politely, which towers have bells that no longer ring. Nobody
  understands the question yet.
- Carts of people from Loon at the eastern gate, with a tower's worth of nothing to say about their bells.
- The second lead after the bells ring again: east as well as west.
- The Kludde's nest: the tallies are already there. Add a bell rope, cut, from a church the hero has never
  heard of (it is in Loon).
- The faction intros name what each faction would do with a silent bell.
- Het Oude Volk's questline ends by pointing at Loon and names the guardians, not the water.
- The Kroniek entry on the Belfort of Ghent and its dragon exists from the start, as plain history.

**From chapter 2 on:** the ledger and the tallies in every town. **Chapter 3:** the loan; Fiere Margriet.
**Chapter 4:** Lenaert's drowned village, in his own account books. **Chapter 5:** the fate of De Stomme.
**Chapter 6:** the rider of 1377 is named by the Oude Volk.

## 7. Bosses

- **Chapter bosses** have phases, an arena, tells for every heavy attack, and a part fought from the dragon
  wherever the boss is big enough. Each drops one named piece of gear and one mark on the dragon.
- **World bosses** appear in a region on a calendar that is the same for every player, so everyone meets the
  same giant in the same week. Factions that trust the hero send fighters. A kill gives a share card.
- Every boss comes from recorded folklore or from the story above, and the Kroniek says which.

## 8. After the ending

The west opens and shows what the choice did: a guarded coast, a drowned polder, or tide lands. What stays to do:
the weekly world boss, the daily bell round in every belfry, harder returns of each chapter boss, each faction's
last tier, the Kroniek, and the marks and colours bosses leave on the dragon.

## 9. To verify before it is written into the game

The artillery's right to bells; the gun foundry and bell founders of Mechelen in 1650; why Sint-Pieter's towers
in Leuven were not finished; Fiere Margriet (1225); the closing of the Scheldt and the Dutch forts; an unpaid
garrison in Antwerp's citadel around 1650; the Ros Beiaard and Dendermonde; the Kludde at Dendermonde; Klokke
Roeland and the dragon of 1377; Saeftinghe (1584) and its legend; Lorraine troops in the lands of Liège; the
Wild Hunt in Limburg; Lange Wapper and Druon Antigoon. A period-plausible name for Lenaert.
