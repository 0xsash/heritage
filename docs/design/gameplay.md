# BELFORT gameplay design (v0.2)

**Brabant, 1650. An open-world action RPG.** You fly a dragon between the real towns of the Spanish Netherlands, land, walk their streets, take on work from their people, and decide how their troubles end. There is no time travel: the whole game takes place in one year, 1650, and the world changes because of what you do in it.

v0.1 was built around short repeating loops. That was the wrong foundation: loops keep people busy, but **choices, consequences and a world worth learning** keep them playing. This version is built around those, and the loops serve them.

---

## 1. The promise

1. **A real country you can fly across.** Diest, Scherpenheuvel, Aarschot, Leuven, Mechelen, Lier and Antwerp at launch, each laid out as it was in the 17th century, joined by the real rivers and roads.
2. **People with their own problems.** Every quest starts with someone who wants something. No quest is a fetch list; each one asks you to decide what is right, or what is profitable, and the town remembers which.
3. **One true year.** The war with France, the closed Scheldt, the Peace of Münster, pilgrims at Scherpenheuvel, Jansenists at Leuven: all real. The fantasy (the Kludde, the drowned sea, the bells that hold it back) lives in the gaps the chronicles left.
4. **Flight is the verb.** Travel, scouting, combat, chases and puzzles all happen in the air as well as on the ground.

---

## 2. The world: seven towns, one country

The map is the Duchy of Brabant and the Lordship of Mechelen, from the Demer to the Scheldt. Towns are full-scale and walkable; the countryside between them is compressed so a flight from Diest to Antwerp takes a few minutes, not an hour.

| Town | In 1650 | What it gives the player | Folklore it hides |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diest** | Cloth town under an absent Orange baron. The bells have stopped. | Start of the story. The weavers, the beguines, the drossaard. | The Kludde in the Demer meadows. |
| **Scherpenheuvel** | Brabant's great pilgrimage. The seven-sided basilica stands on the oak's spot. | Pilgrims bring news from everywhere: the rumour mill. Oak relics. | The oak's roots under the altar are still alive. |
| **Aarschot** | Duchy of the Arenbergs, on the Demer. | First road town. Deserters camp in the woods. | Will-o'-the-wisps on the marsh roads. |
| **Leuven** | The old university. Jansenists against Jesuits. Brewers. | Scholars read the inscriptions on bells and old wood. Books, lore, skill training. | A professor who knows too much about the sea. |
| **Mechelen** | Seat of the Great Council. Bell-founders. | Justice (trials you can sway). Bell-founding: your bell magic is forged here. | The founders' oldest mould. |
| **Lier** | Small, rich, on the Nete. | Quiet side stories, a resting place. | The town that sleeps through everything. |
| **Antwerp** | Its river closed by the peace of 1648. Still rich in painters, printers and the Spanish citadel. | The big city: art, money, smuggling, espionage. | Druon Antigoon, the giant who once took a hand from every skipper. |

Later expansions: Brussels (the governor's court), Ghent, Bruges, the coast.

**The countryside** is not empty space. Between towns you find villages and farms, abbeys (Averbode, Tongerlo), wayside shrines, gallows hills, toll houses, deserters' camps, and the places the quests send you: a mill, a drowned church, a heath where the shepherds will not graze.

---

## 3. The main story: *The Year the Bells Fell Silent*

Real history is the frame; the fantasy explains what history cannot.

**The premise.** The low country was taken from the sea, and the sea wants it back. For centuries the bells of Brabant's towns, rung at the hours, have held it back. Nobody alive knows this. Now, one town at a time, the bells are going silent: their clappers stolen, their bronze cracked, their ringers drowned in shallow ditches. Something that smells of river water is behind it.

**Act I: Diest (built).** The bells of Sint-Sulpitius stop ringing. The trail of wet weed and a pilgrim badge leads to Scherpenheuvel, to the roots of the felled oak that still hum under the basilica, and to a tall man in a long coat who has been buying the oak's wood. The act ends with the trail pointing west, to Leuven.

**Act II: the road west (Aarschot, Leuven, Mechelen).**
- In Leuven a theology professor reads the inscription on an oak statuette and is found drowned the next morning in a dry cellar.
- The rival factions (Jansenists, Jesuits) each want what you found and each tell you a different story.
- In Mechelen the bell-founders can cast a bell that cannot be silenced, but they need bronze from a bell that has already been silenced.
- The Great Council puts someone on trial for the drownings. Your evidence decides the verdict.

**Act III: Antwerp and the Scheldt.**
- The river closed in 1648, and the drowned sea wants it opened.
- The man in the long coat is the Kludde wearing a human face. He has been working with a party in Antwerp that profits from a ruined countryside.
- The last confrontation happens over the Scheldt at night, with every bell you saved ringing at once.

**Endings depend on what you did.** Which towns got their bells back, which factions trust you, whom you spared, and whether you told the truth at the Great Council. Each one changes who stands with you in the final act and what happens to the towns afterwards.

## 4. Quests: what depth means here

### 4.1 Kinds of quests

| Kind | What it is | Example (Diest) |
|---|---|---|
| **Main quest** | The story. Always tracked unless you choose otherwise. | *The Bells That Will Not Ring* |
| **Town quest** | A person's problem, with branches and consequences. Several per town. | *Too Many Teeth*: a widow forging the town's cloth seals |
| **Contract** | Paid work from a notice board or an official. Quick and repeatable in kind, never in text. | *A Letter for the Provost* |
| **Mystery** | You gather clues in the world and in conversations, then name the culprit. You can be wrong. | The Leuven drowning (Act II) |
| **Bounty** | A named enemy with a home, habits and a reason. | A deserter captain raiding farms near Aarschot |
| **Companion quest** | Unlocks with trust. Changes what the companion can do and how the story ends. | (Act II) |
| **World event** | Happens at a time and place whether or not you are there: a procession, a fire, a raid, a market day. Being there opens a quest; not being there has consequences. | The great procession to Scherpenheuvel |

### 4.2 Rules every quest follows

1. **It starts with a person**, never a board with no one behind it. Even contracts have a client with a face.
2. **At least one real decision.** Not "good or evil", but two goods, or two prices: the law or mercy, the guild or the widow, the toll or the smuggler's friendship.
3. **The decision shows.** Someone says so, a price changes, a door opens or closes, a person appears somewhere else later. Already built in Diest:
   - If you turn Lijsbet over to the drossaard, the weavers pay well and she is banished.
   - If you let her run, she gives you her seal mould. She will turn up in Antwerp in Act III, in debt to the wrong people.
   - If you send her to the beguines, the Begijnhof trusts you and the guild trusts you a little less.
4. **More than one way to solve it.** Talk, fight, sneak, bribe, or use the dragon (frighten, carry, spot from above).
5. **It teaches the world.** Every quest is set somewhere real and is about something people really argued about in 1650: guild rights, tolls, war deserters, relics, closed rivers.
6. **Tone:** the main story is dark and true. Town quests are allowed to be mischievous: a smuggler who swears his brandy is vinegar, a relic seller whose chips of the true oak "have worked several miracles each".

### 4.3 The journal (built)

- Opens with **J** (RB on a gamepad).
- Lists quests under **Main**, **Quests**, **Contracts** and **Completed**.
- Each quest shows:
  - its current objectives, with counts;
  - its reward;
  - the full entry, newest first, written in the first person, as a diary of 1650;
  - **Track / untrack**.
- The tracked objective appears on the compass, the map and the on-screen tracker.
- The journal also shows your purse, your renown per town and what you are carrying.

There are no distance markers, no minimap arrows inside buildings, and no modern units. People give directions the way they did in 1650: "past the Cow Gate", "west along the Aarschot road; you will see the dome".

## 5. Factions and reputation

Reputation is tracked **per town** (renown, built) and **per faction** (next). They pull against each other on purpose.

| Faction | Wants | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **The Crown** (the governor, garrisons, drossaards) | Order, taxes, tolls, deserters hanged | Passes through gates at night, legal pardons, soldiers in the finale |
| **The Church** (chapters, Jesuits, the Scherpenheuvel provost) | Relics protected, heresy found | Sanctuary, relic lore, healing |
| **The Guilds** (weavers, brewers, bell-founders) | Their monopolies kept | Prices, crafted gear, the unsilenceable bell |
| **The Beguines** | To be left alone, and widows sheltered | Healing salves, herb lore, a network of houses in every town |
| **The University** (Leuven) | Books and the truth, preferably first | Skill training, translations, the meaning of the inscriptions |
| **The Brandy Road** (smugglers and carters) | The toll unpaid | Black-market goods, secret routes, informants |

High standing with the Crown makes the smugglers close their doors, and so on. The game never says one side is right.

## 6. Growing stronger

### The rider
- **Three paths, mixed freely, unlocked by doing rather than by points:**
  - **Steel:** sword, buckler and pistol. Parries, ripostes, finishers.
  - **Bell:** the signature magic (see §7). Learned from the beguines, deepened at Leuven, forged at Mechelen.
  - **Tongue:** persuasion, bribery, lying, reading people. Opens dialogue options and alternative solutions.
- **Gear** comes from people, not from random drops:
  - a Mechelen armourer's buckler;
  - a Leuven scholar's spectacles, which reveal hidden marks;
  - Antwerp's printed maps, which reveal secrets on the map.
  - Each piece has a story and a choice of upgrades.

### The dragon
- **It grows over the year:** hatchling, to young, to grown. Wingspan, speed and what it can carry (a person, a cask, a bell) change with it.
- **Breath and the harness** are upgraded through the story and the guilds.
- **The dragon has a temperament.** Feed it, fly it hard and let it hunt, and it gains confidence. Neglect it and it sulks: it is slower to answer your call. Its trust gates the most dangerous flights.
- **Dragon upkeep is real money.** It eats a sheep a day. Farmers have opinions about that.

## 7. Combat

- **On the ground:**
  - close, weighty sword fighting;
  - parry windows, stagger and finishers;
  - one or two enemies attack at once while the rest circle (attack tokens).
- **In the air:**
  - dive strikes, tail sweeps and breath;
  - you land hits on the wing and pull out before the counter.
- **Bell magic** ties the two together:
  - Hitting on the beat builds **Resonance**.
  - Spending it rings one of three bells, and the bells combine like chords:
    - **low** stuns,
    - **middle** wards,
    - **high** reveals, for example a Kludde in disguise.
  - Saved belfries add new bells.
- **Enemies:**
  - deserters and free companies from the French war;
  - smugglers;
  - the Kludde in its many shapes (dog, horse, crow, eel);
  - drowned ringers;
  - will-o'-the-wisps;
  - things the sea sends up the rivers.
- **Juice from the first prototype:** hitstop, camera trauma, impact sound layers, and enemies that telegraph. See `docs/research/game-design-talks.md`.

---

## 8. Economy and trade

- **Money:** florins and stivers (20 stivers to the florin).
- **Each town makes something and wants something.** Diest has cloth, Leuven beer, Mechelen bronze and furniture, Antwerp books, paintings and colonial goods brought in overland. Prices differ by town and shift with events: a raid on the Aarschot road makes cloth dear in Leuven.
- **The dragon can carry trade goods,** so you can be a merchant if you like. Tolls apply at every gate, unless you know the Brandy Road.
- **Money buys** gear, dragon upkeep, bribes, information and a room for the night. Spending it is a choice, never a chore.

---

## 9. A world that lives without you

- **Days have a shape.**
  - Gates open at dawn and close at dusk.
  - Markets run on market days.
  - Beguines ring their own small bells.
  - Taverns fill at night, and the Kludde hunts after dark.
- **Bells tell the time.** In a town whose bells are silent, people lose track of the hours. Shops open late and the gates close at odd times. That is how the main story shows up in daily life.
- **People have routines** (work, church, tavern, home) and they remember you. They greet you by name, gossip about what you did, and refuse to talk to you if you sold out their friend.
- **Rumours travel with pilgrims.** What you did in Diest is talked about in Scherpenheuvel the next day, and in Leuven a few days later.
- **World events** are on a calendar: processions, fairs, a fire, a flood, a public execution. Some can be prevented, some only witnessed.

## 10. Why people come back (depth, not loops)

1. **"What happens if I choose differently?"** Branching quests with visible outcomes make a second playthrough a different story.
2. **"What is over there?"** Seven real towns to learn, each with its own secrets, plus the countryside between them. Exploration is rewarded with lore, quests and gear, never with empty collectibles.
3. **"Who did it?"** Mysteries you solve yourself, with clues you can miss and an accusation you can get wrong.
4. **"I'm getting better at this."** Flight and combat that reward mastery: dive lines, parry timing, chord combinations.
5. **"My dragon."** A companion that grows and has moods.
6. **"This really happened."** Players learn something true about Flanders, and the fantasy makes them want to.

The short loops still exist inside all of this: spot something from the air, land, deal with it, take off. They are the rhythm, not the reason.

## 11. What is built, and what is next

**Built (playable now):**
- **The whole game is in Flemish**, with the 17th-century "gij".
- **The front end:**
  - a title screen;
  - five painted story slides;
  - character creation: name, one of three origins, cloak colour, your dragon's name.
- **The origins:**
  - *Schuttersgezel* (sword);
  - *Klokkenluiderskind* (bell magic);
  - *Smokkelaarsjong* (pistol and smoke).
- **Combat:**
  - a three-hit sword combo with buffering and target assist;
  - a dodge roll you can't be hit during;
  - two skills per origin;
  - dragon fire from the air;
  - hitstop, white flashes, knockback, damage numbers.
- **Enemies:**
  - telegraphs and attack tokens, at most two swinging at once;
  - leashing back to their camp;
  - elites with a boss bar.
- **The game layer:**
  - XP and levels;
  - four loot rarities, a bag, three gear slots;
  - a shop, potions, death and respawn.
- **Townsfolk:** a recycled crowd walks the real streets. They greet you, gossip about your choices, know your name once you are famous, and scatter when the dragon lands.
- **Guidance:**
  - one tracked objective, shown as a pillar of light in the world;
  - a ground circle for areas to search or clear;
  - an on-screen waypoint that clamps to the edge;
  - a three-quest tracker;
  - quest givers' ! and ? markers, readable from the air;
  - map marks for quest givers and enemy camps;
  - banners for new quests, completed quests and level-ups.
- **Quests:**
  - the prologue and Act I of the main story: the stolen clappers, the road to Scherpenheuvel, the hunt for the Kludde, and the bells ringing again;
  - five side quests with branching endings, among them a bounty and a wisp hunt;
  - one contract;
  - seven enemy camps spread out by level.
- **The world:** Diest and Scherpenheuvel in 1650.
- **Data, not yet in the game:** six more towns (`public/world/regions/`).

**Next, in order:**
1. **Multi-town world:** stream Aarschot, Leuven, Mechelen, Lier and Antwerp into the game. The data is ready in `public/world/regions/`: compressed countryside, rivers, roads, villages.
2. **Combat depth:** a dive strike from the dragon, elite affixes, a talent choice at each level, and night-only Kludde hunts.
3. **Act II in Leuven:** a mystery quest, the faction split, and the first faction reputation.
4. **NPC routines and gossip:** day schedules, and "they remember you" lines driven by flags.
5. **Trade and dragon upkeep.**
6. **Accounts and cloud saves** (Supabase), and tracking which choices people make.
